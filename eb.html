Banker · usually Q1The Four Noble Truths ★★★

Asked 2020, 2021, 2022 (×2), 2023, 2024. Angles: "name them and describe one", "validity of suffering today", "suffering leads to happiness", "the 3rd/4th truth and Nibbāna", "why noble", "neither pessimistic nor optimistic".

Explain it simply

The Buddha is like a doctor. He doesn't pretend you're fine (that's not optimism), and he doesn't say you'll die (that's not pessimism). He's realistic: here's the illness, here's the cause, there is a cure, here's the medicine.

TruthPāliDoctorYour job
1DukkhaDiagnosis: birth, ageing, sickness, death; not getting what you wantUnderstand it
2Dukkha-samudayaCause: craving (taṇhā) for sense pleasures, for existence, for non-existenceAbandon it
3Dukkha-nirodhaCure: craving ends → suffering ends = NibbānaRealise it
4MaggaMedicine: the Noble Eightfold PathDevelop it
Memory tricks

The four: Dukkha · Samudaya · Nirodha · Magga, “Don't Stop, Never Mind”. Doctor order: Sick → Why → Cure → Medicine.

The four jobs: “You Are Really Done”: Understand · Abandon · Realise · Develop.

Why noble: “Triple A: true at Any time, for Anyone, Anywhere.” Unlike “white” or “tall”, which depend on comparison.

3 cravings: “Want it, Be it, Be rid of it”: sense pleasures (kāma-taṇhā), existence (bhava-taṇhā), non-existence (vibhava-taṇhā).

“Is suffering still valid today?” (2023, 2024)

Yes. Modern examples of each craving: wanting more phones, food and likes (kāma); wanting to be rich, famous, young forever (bhava); wanting to get rid of pain, people, even life (vibhava). Stress, anxiety, debt and burnout are the same dukkha in new clothes. The cause is the same: craving.

BankerThe three characteristics ★★★

Asked every year 2020–2024. Angles: "universal", "inter-relationship", "the world is continuously changing", "as depicted in the Sutta Piṭaka".

Explain it simply

Think of a sandcastle on the beach. It keeps changing and will be washed away (anicca, impermanent). Because it can't last, loving it brings tears when the wave comes (dukkha, unsatisfactory). And you can't order it to stay, so it isn't really “yours” (anattā, not-self). Everything in life is a sandcastle, including your body and feelings.

MarkWhat it isWhy it's trueDhp
AniccaEverything changesEverything depends on conditions; conditions change277
DukkhaUnsatisfactory, painfulWhat changes can't give lasting happiness278
AnattāNo permanent self or soulWe can't control the body: “let it be thus” fails279
Memory tricks “I Don't Own it”

Impermanence · Dukkha · nOt-self. And the verses are in a row: Dhp 277, 278, 279.

How they link: “Changes → Can't satisfy → Can't be me.” What is impermanent is unsatisfactory; what is impermanent and unsatisfactory is not-self. This chain answers every “inter-relationship” question.

Quote to learnSabbe saṅkhārā aniccā · sabbe saṅkhārā dukkhā · sabbe dhammā anattā

All conditioned things are impermanent · all conditioned things are suffering · all things are not-self (Dhp 277–279). Note: the third says dhammā (all things), not saṅkhārā.

Banker · not on the principal's list!Dependent origination ★★★

Asked every year 2020–2024. Angles: "existence in saṁsāra", "who sees paṭiccasamuppāda sees the Dhamma", "special features", "the essence of Buddhist teaching", "how the 12 links are causally related", "theory of causality".

Explain it simply

Paṭicca-samuppāda means “arising in dependence”. Nothing pops up on its own. Everything happens because of something else, like a row of dominoes. Ignorance tips the first domino, and one by one they fall until we get birth, ageing and death, again and again (saṁsāra). Take one domino away and the chain stops. That's the whole idea.

The rule in one lineImasmiṁ sati idaṁ hoti; imassuppādā idaṁ uppajjati.

“When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises.” Like: no fuel, no fire.

The 12 links

  1. Avijjā ignorancepast life
  2. Saṅkhāra volitional formationspast life (kamma)
  3. Viññāṇa consciousnessthis life
  4. Nāmarūpa name-and-formmind and body
  5. Saḷāyatana six sense baseseye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind
  6. Phassa contactsense meets object
  7. Vedanā feelingpleasant, painful, neutral
  8. Taṇhā cravingbreak the chain here!
  9. Upādāna clinging
  10. Bhava becoming
  11. Jāti birthnext life
  12. Jarā-maraṇa ageing and deathnext life

Grey = past life · teal = this life · orange = future life. The dashed boxes are where practice cuts the chain: feeling → craving. Be mindful of a feeling and craving doesn't have to follow.

Memory trick: the 12 in order In Fog, Careless Nuns Spill Coffee; Feeling Cranky, Clumsy, Bumping Bowls Again

Ignorance · Formations · Consciousness · Name-and-form · Six senses · Contact · Feeling · Craving · Clinging · Becoming · Birth · Ageing and death. “Fog” = ignorance, and “Again” = the cycle starts again.

Memory trick: the 4 special features (2022) True · Must · Always · Because
  • True = objective (tathatā): a law of nature whether Buddhas appear or not
  • Must = necessary (avitathatā): with the cause, the effect must come
  • Always = invariable (anaññathatā): same cause, same effect, every time
  • Because = conditionality (idappaccayatā): this happens because of that
Why “who sees dependent origination sees the Dhamma” (MN 28)

Because it explains all the other teachings at once: the 2nd truth (how suffering arises, links 1→12), the 3rd truth (how it ends, in reverse), not-self (a chain of causes, no soul needed), kamma and rebirth (links 2 and 10), and the Middle Way: not eternalism (a permanent soul) and not annihilationism (nothing after death).

BankerThe Noble Eightfold Path ★★★

Asked every year 2020–2024. Angles: "practicability for Nibbāna", "applied in daily life", "evaluate as the path to Nibbāna", "leading to realisation".

Explain it simply

The path is a GPS route to Nibbāna. But the route passes through lovely towns: even if you're still far from the end, life gets better at every step. That's the principal's point: “until the final goal, we still have to live.” The path makes today peaceful too.

The 8 factors are like strands of one rope, not steps on a ladder. You practise them all together.

The groups: “2 – 3 – 3” = Wisdom 2 · Morality 3 · Concentration 3. Think Head (wisdom), Hands (morality), Heart (training the mind).

Banker · starred by the principalKamma and intention ★★★

Asked 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024 (+ inequality 2021). Angles: "cetanāhaṁ… comment", "volition is kamma", "wholesome and unwholesome", "does kamma divide beings into high and low?"

Explain it simply

Kamma is not “bad luck”. It just means action, and what makes an action count is the intention behind it. Think of a seed: your intention is the seed, and the result is the fruit that grows later. An accident with no intention plants no seed.

Stepping on an ant by accident = no kamma of killing. Moving someone's 10-cent coin meaning to take it = stealing, because the intention is there.

The quote to learn (AN 6.63)Cetanāhaṁ bhikkhave kammaṁ vadāmi

“Monks, it is intention that I call kamma.” Pali you already know: bhikkhave = “O monks!” (vocative) · vadāmi = “I say” (1st person, -mi = me).

Memory tricks

The quote: “Chetana-hum: I call kamma intention.”

Buddha vs Jains: “Jains watch the feet; the Buddha watches the heart.” Jain monks sweep the path and filter water because for them the bodily act counts even by accident. The Buddha said intention counts most (Upāli Sutta, MN 56).

Kamma is not fate: not everything is past kamma. Weather, illness and carelessness also play a part (Sīvaka Sutta).

“Does kamma make beings high or low?” (2021, 2022)

Yes, but by action, not birth. In the Cūḷakammavibhaṅga Sutta (MN 135) young Subha asks why some people are short-lived or long-lived, sick or healthy, poor or rich. The Buddha says: “Beings are owners of their kamma, heirs of their kamma.” Killing → short life; kindness → long life; generosity → wealth. And in the Vāseṭṭha Sutta: one is noble or low by deeds, not by caste.

Backup · overlaps Social DimensionsThe five precepts ★★★

Asked 2021, 2023, 2024. Angles: "significant for Buddhists and non-Buddhists", "universal code for world peace".

Explain it simply

The precepts are a promise you make to yourself, not a law with police. The words say it: sikkhāpadaṁ samādiyāmi = “I undertake the training rule”. If you break one, nobody throws you out. You start again, like Aṅgulimāla, who killed many people and still became an arahant.

They work for everyone because the reason is simple: I don't want to be hurt, so others don't either.

Memory tricks

The five: “Kind Souls Seldom Lie Drunk”: Killing · Stealing · Sexual misconduct · Lying · Drink and drugs.

Not rules: “A promise, not a prison.”

World peace (2021, 2023): “Five gifts of fearlessness”: keep them and you give countless beings freedom from fear (AN 8.39). 1 person safe → family safe → country safe → world at peace.

Principal's questionThe ten wholesome and unwholesome deeds ★★

Explain it simply

Your actions leave through three doors: your body, your mouth and your mind. The ten bad deeds are the ways harm gets out through each door. The ten good deeds are simply not doing them, and doing the opposite.

DoorUnwholesomeWholesome opposite

Memory tricks

The shape: “3 – 4 – 3, like a phone number”. Speech gets the most because words do so much harm.

The ten: “KSS · Little Snakes Hiss Idly · 3 Cs”: Kill, Steal, Sex · Lie, Slander, Harsh, Idle · Covet, Curse (ill will), Confused view.

Bonus link: the 3 mind deeds are the 3 roots: covetousness = greed, ill will = hatred, wrong view = delusion.

Backup · also a short noteThe five aggregates ★★

Asked 2020, 2024 as essays; 2022, 2023 as short notes. Angles: "constituents of an individual", "empirical existence (khandha-āyatana-dhātu)".

Explain it simply

A car is just parts: wheels, engine, seats. There's no extra thing called “car” hiding inside. In the same way, “I” is just 5 changing parts: a body, feelings, labels, reactions and awareness. The Buddha compared it to a chariot (Vajirā Sutta).

AggregateSimple meaningSimile (SN 22.95)
Form rūpaThe body (earth, water, fire, air)A lump of foam
Feeling vedanāPleasant, painful, neutralA water bubble
Perception saññāRecognising, labellingA mirage
Formations saṅkhāraIntentions, habits (kamma made here)A banana trunk
Consciousness viññāṇaKnowing through 6 sensesA magic trick
Memory tricks

The five: “Funny Frogs Paint Fancy Castles”: Form · Feeling · Perception · Formations · Consciousness.

The similes: “Foam Bubbles Make Bananas Magic”: all look solid, none has a core.

The sutta name explains itself: Pheṇa-piṇḍa-upama = foam · lump · simile.

Nāma and rūpa: form = rūpa (body); the other four = nāma (mind). Together: nāmarūpa (a common short note).

BackupNibbāna and its two types ★★

Asked 2020, 2022, 2023 as “Nibbāna is the supreme bliss” (nibbānaṁ paramaṁ sukhaṁ).

Explain it simply

Nibbāna means “blowing out”, like a candle flame going out when the wax is finished. What goes out? The three fires: greed, hatred and delusion. It isn't a heaven and it isn't “nothing”. It's the peace left when the fires are gone.

With remainder: the fire is out but the candle stick is still there. The Buddha at 35 still had a body. Without remainder: the candle stick is gone too. The Buddha passed away at 80 (parinibbāna).

TypePāliWhenThe Buddha
With remaindersa-upādisesaDuring life: defilements gone, body remainsBodhgayā, age 35
Without remainderanupādisesaAt death: the aggregates end too, no rebirthKusinārā, age 80
Memory tricks

The two types: “With body, without body”. sa = with, an = without. It's the same an as in an-āgāmī, “non-returner”!

The ages: “35 sa, 80 an”.

What it ends: “Blow out the three fires: Greed, Hate, Fog.”

Why supreme bliss: every other happiness is anicca and ends; Nibbāna is unconditioned (asaṅkhata), so it can't end.

Principal's question · also a short noteBuddhist meditation ★★

Explain it simply

Imagine a glass of muddy water. Samatha (calm) is letting it sit still until the mud settles. Vipassanā (insight) is then looking through the clear water and seeing the bottom: seeing things as they really are (impermanent, unsatisfactory, not-self).

Samatha already existed before the Buddha (he learned it from Āḷāra Kālāma and Uddaka Rāmaputta). Vipassanā is his own discovery.

Memory tricks

The two: “S is for Still; V is for View”.

What insight sees: “I Don't Own it”: impermanence, dukkha, not-self.

The threefold training: sīla → samādhi → paññā = “Behave, Breathe, Believe-what-you-see”. Morality is the foundation for a calm mind.

Principal's questionWhat is Early Buddhism? ★★

Asked 2022: “Introduce Early Buddhism and clarify its main teachings.”

Explain it simply

Early Buddhism is the original recipe: what the Buddha and his first disciples taught, before different “restaurants” (schools) added their own sauces. Every restaurant still uses the same core recipe (Four Truths, Path, three characteristics, kamma), so we can't say any one school is Early Buddhism.

Memory tricks

The language: “Māgadhī Mouth, Pāli Later, Sanskrit Script”. The Buddha spoke Māgadhī; the name “Pāli” came later in Sri Lanka; Sanskrit was later used to write texts down.

The 4 timeline views: “No Rules · No Abhidhamma · No Rituals · No Rival vehicles”. First 20 years = before Vinaya rules · up to the 1st Council = before the Abhidhamma · up to the 2nd Council = before rituals and the first split · up to the Theravāda/Mahāyāna split.

The features: “Practice, Proof, Personal effort”: practice not ritual · free inquiry (Kālāma Sutta) · “Buddhas only show the way” (Dhp 276).

Backup · also a short noteThe five hindrances ★★

Asked 2021, 2023, 2024 (“defilements and purifying the mind”), 2022 as a note.

Explain it simply

Imagine trying to see your face in a bowl of water. The Buddha said each hindrance spoils the water in a different way, so you can't see clearly (AN 5.193):

HindrancePāliThe water is…Remedy
Sensual desirekāmacchandaDyed with colourReflect on impermanence and unattractiveness

Sloth & torporthīna-middhaCovered in mossEffort, walking, light, fresh air

Memory tricks

The five: “Some Angry Sleepy Restless Doubters”: Sensual desire · Anger (ill will) · Sloth · Restlessness · Doubt.

The water: “Colour, Boil, Moss, Wind, Mud”.

Freedom from them feels like (DN 2): a debt repaid, recovering from illness, release from prison, freedom from slavery, crossing a desert safely.

Two general ways to remove them (2023 asks for two): 1. Mindfulness: notice the hindrance and name it. 2. Its specific opposite (the remedy column). Also: keep the precepts, guard the senses, good friends.

Backup · also a short noteThe four illimitables (brahmavihāra) ★★

Asked 2023 (“practised in daily life”), notes in 2021, 2023, 2024.

Explain it simply

Think of how a good mother feels about her child at different times:

  • A baby: she wishes it well → mettā (loving-kindness)
  • A sick child: she wants its pain to end → karuṇā (compassion)
  • A child who wins a prize: she is happy for it → muditā (sympathetic joy)
  • A grown-up child living its own life: she stays calm and lets go → upekkhā (equanimity)

They're called illimitable (appamaññā) because you spread them to all beings, without limit.

QualityDaily lifeFar enemyNear enemy (looks similar)
MettāSmile, kind words, wish people wellIll willSelfish attachment
KaruṇāHelp someone in troubleCrueltyPity, sad grief
MuditāCelebrate a colleague's promotionEnvyOver-excitement
UpekkhāStay balanced in praise and blameGreed and resentmentNot caring
Memory tricks

The four: “Make Kind Moments Unlimited”: Mettā · Karuṇā · Muditā · Upekkhā.

The mother picture: “Baby, Sick, Prize, Grown-up”.

Helps many answersThe noble ones and the four stages

Explain it simply

Ariya = noble, by wisdom, not by birth. Picture Nibbāna as the far shore of a river:

StagePāliPictureLives left
Stream-entererSotāpannaStepped into the river; can't go backAt most 7
Once-returnerSakadāgāmīComes back to this world once1
Non-returnerAnāgāmīNever returns here; finishes in a pure realm0 here
ArahantArahantReached the far shoreNone
Memory tricks

Countdown: “7 → 1 → 0 → free”. Words: sota + āpanna = stream-entered · saka + āgāmī = once-comer · an + āgāmī = not-comer.

Ten fetters: stream-enterer cuts 3 (self-view, doubt, clinging to rituals) · once-returner weakens greed and anger · non-returner cuts them (5 gone) · arahant cuts all 10.

Your Saṅgha chant: 4 stages × (path + fruit) = cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā, “four pairs, eight individuals”.
