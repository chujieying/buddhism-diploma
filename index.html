How to study with this site

A little every day beats a lot the night before.

  1. LearnOne lesson a day. Read the simple explanation out loud and learn its memory trick.
  2. CardsTen minutes of flashcards on everything you've learned so far. Be honest with “Again”.
  3. QuizTen questions. Score 9 out of 10 three times in a row and that topic is ready.
  4. EssaysIn the last week, plan one real past question a day in five minutes, then compare with the outline.
A friendly warning, not examinable

Don't collect the Dhamma like souvenirs

The Tibetan teacher Chögyam Trungpa had a name for a trap every keen student falls into: spiritual materialism. In Cutting Through Spiritual Materialism (1973) he warned that the ego is very clever. It can turn anything into a possession, even the teachings meant to loosen its grip.

And this website is, let's be honest, a very tidy display cabinet. Twelve links of dependent origination, neatly labelled. Five hindrances in a row. A mnemonic for every list. It is perfectly possible to finish every flashcard deck, score 10 out of 10 on every quiz, and come out with a bigger ego and a shinier collection of Buddhist artefacts than before.

If you can recite all five hindrances but still snap at the bus driver, the ego has passed the exam, and you haven't.

Cittassa damatho sādhu,
cittaṁ dantaṁ sukhāvahaṁ. “Good is the taming of the mind; a tamed mind brings happiness.” Dhammapada 35

That verse is from the Citta Vagga, which is also in the Pāli syllabus. So yes, you can memorise it for marks. But its real point is the opposite of memorising: the practice is taming the mind, not filling it. The diploma tests what you know. Life tests what you've tamed.

So study well, pass well, and then close the laptop and sit for ten minutes. Watch the breath. Notice the craving to open just one more flashcard. That noticing is the actual curriculum.
